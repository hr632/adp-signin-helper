What it does

Monitors for the spinner
ADP's sign-in page spins forever when the cookies set on adp.com outgrow what ADP's servers accept; they answer HTTP 431. The extension watches every adp.com response. On a 431 it deletes only the bot-detection, telemetry and branding cookies in that window (no sign-in depends on them) and reloads that one tab. Other tabs are never touched. At most once per 30 seconds per tab.
Clears everything on your click
"Clear all ADP cookies and reload" in the popup deletes every adp.com cookie in that window and reloads the tab. This signs you out of other ADP products open in the same window, so it is never automatic.
Installs Touch ID on the sign-in screen
ADP offers Touch ID only where it finds its own enrollment record in the browser profile's local storage: your user ID, the passkey's ID, an account ID, a device ID. It never checks the keychain. When the sign-in screen (online.adp.com/signin) loads, the extension writes the records it holds into that profile if they are missing. Nothing happens on any other page.
Saves records on your click
"Save Touch ID records from this screen" reads the records from a sign-in screen in a profile where Touch ID already works and keeps them in your Google account through Chrome sync. Only on click, never automatically.

What it never does

No passwords, fingerprints or private keys: the key stays in your keychain and the record alone cannot sign anyone in. No outside servers. No site other than adp.com. No navigation, no opening of tabs. No deletion of session cookies without your click.

Limits

It cannot enroll Touch ID. ADP's enrollment page is broken in Chrome and Safari, so a profile where Touch ID already works is the source. Records are per person; a coworker needs their own. Chrome sync must be on for records to follow your Google account; otherwise they stay in this profile.
